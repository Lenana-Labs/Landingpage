01 / Our story

A studio taking shape.

LenanaLabs exists for problems that do not arrive neatly packaged — the ones with a constraint attached, a process nobody has written down, and a result that has to hold up outside a demo.

Three people are building the studio around that work: engineering for clients, products of our own, and research that keeps both honest. Client work brings constraints we could not invent. Products make us live with our own decisions. Research stops the other two from becoming habit.

Being early is a fact rather than a theme. It means fewer layers between the person who asks the question and the person who answers it, and it means we choose work we can do properly rather than work that merely looks impressive.

We are based in Nairobi and work with teams wherever they are. What we can offer is attention: to the problem, to the constraints, and to the person who has to use what we build.

02 / What we believe

Four positions we keep returning to.

Draft messaging — proposed principles, not yet official company values

  1. 01

    Understand before building.

    The problem is the brief. If we cannot describe how the current system works — including the workaround someone invented to survive it — we are not ready to replace anything.

  2. 02

    Experiment before committing.

    The cheapest place to be wrong is a prototype, not a contract. We would rather spend a week disproving an idea than a quarter discovering it in production.

  3. 03

    Build for reality.

    Real users, real infrastructure, real budgets. A solution that needs conditions nobody has is a demonstration, not a solution.

  4. 04

    Keep learning.

    Every build should leave the next one easier. Engineering is iterative, and so is our understanding of the problem.

03 / How we think and work

Most of the work is the cutting.

A normal week is six approaches, five rejections and one build. The five steps below are the ones we actually repeat; the plate shows what the deciding looks like when it is written down.

  1. 01 — Understand

    Read the situation

    The process, the constraint, the person holding it together.

  2. 02 — Explore

    Widen the options

    Alternatives on purpose, including the unfashionable ones.

  3. 03 — Prototype

    Make it cheap to be wrong

    The smallest version that could disprove the idea.

  4. 04 — Engineer

    Build the survivor

    Clear interfaces, tests where they matter, nothing unmaintainable.

  5. 05 — Evaluate

    Check against the problem

    Keep what changed. Remove what merely shipped.

Most branches die between steps 02 and 03. The rest of the work is deciding which one deserves the build.

…and then again, usually with a better question

04 / What we explore

Three activities, one loop.

Client engineering, internal products and research are not separate departments. What we learn in one changes how we work in the others — a method, a pattern, a way of testing. Skills travel between them; client code, data and confidential detail do not travel without permission.
